The local key is stored with these fingerprints, so this is **not encryption** or a promise that someone with the saved data cannot guess a short phrase; it avoids retaining plain transcripts. This is a **post-transcription confusion correction**, not retraining Vosk's acoustic weights and not voice biometrics. It helps only when an accent produces an owner-confirmed or very close transcription; it cannot recover speech the microphone did not capture or a wake both decoders missed. Source tests validate matching, consent, bounds and local reset; the owner must measure whether it improves *their* phrases on the Pi without increasing wrong answers. [Vosk documents](https://alphacephei.com/vosk/models) that small models allow dynamic vocabulary/grammar reconfiguration, but that is not equivalent to on-device accent training. A bigger offline recognizer must be benchmarked for latency and RAM before considering replacement on the Pi 4.

The 0.2.5 phrase check changes only the ReSpeaker hardware mixer on obvious quiet/clipped samples. It does not tune the PCM sent to Vosk. 0.2.6 adds a numeric, conservative **pre-recognizer** profile:

1. At the start of a local check, show a four-second quiet-room interval. Its clock begins on the first actual microphone level report, not the UI tap, so a delayed voice service cannot skip it. The guided session allows up to fifteen minutes so ten prompts and owner-confirmed repeats do not expire at an ordinary speaking pace.
   If the microphone reports levels but no complete spoken segment follows for twelve seconds, show a non-failing hint tailored to a quiet signal, clipping, or other activity without a phrase boundary. If the room baseline never arrived, identify that separately instead of suggesting amplification. These hints do not fabricate a recognized phrase, advance the check, or execute commands.
2. Retain only bounded numeric room-floor samples, low-frequency room-noise fraction, and phrase RMS, peak, DC bias, and clipped fraction. No audio or transcript is written to storage. The existing ten positive/negative phrase checks still verify the wake/word path and cannot execute actions.
   A separate acoustic phrase boundary during the check uses raw sound level, a short pre-roll, 750 ms of quiet to end a phrase, and a seven-second limit. It still submits a sample when Vosk emits no words or no endpoint. Outside calibration, this boundary cannot trigger commands.
   Measure speech RMS from the voiced quarter-second frames only. The leading room pre-roll and trailing pause previously diluted this figure and could cause an unnecessary HAT gain increase. Retain the maximum peak and worst clipped-frame fraction from the **entire** segment so removing silence from the level estimate cannot hide a clipped syllable.
3. After three acoustic speech segments, compare median raw speech level with a conservative upper-quartile quiet-room level **even if Vosk recognized no words**. Per-second room reports use the median frame level, not the single quietest moment. A clean but weak signal can receive a temporary trial of at most 2× digital gain, limited per frame so sudden loud sounds do not clip. A persistent DC bias **or repeatable measured low-frequency quiet-room energy** can enable a light 75 Hz high-pass trial. Its room metric is calculated from how much the actual filter suppresses each raw quiet-room frame, not from the spoken phrase's pitch; insufficient samples cannot trigger it. **Any** clipped spoken sample vetoes amplification; a median or a filter that discards damaged samples must not conceal intermittent clipping. If SNR is poor, the source clips, or measurement is insufficient, trial untouched audio and explain the physical fix. Do **not** gate quiet consonants or blindly amplify a noisy room.
   A previously missed edge case was a clean, very faint voice with a raw peak between 0.25% and 0.5% of full scale: the old setup segmenter never created a sample, so neither the bounded hardware-gain step nor a digital trial could run. The 0.2.6 setup-only detector now accepts two consecutive quarter-second frames above the lower 0.25% peak floor **and** the measured room-relative RMS/peak gates. These faint samples remain below the stricter phrase-level pass threshold; their purpose is to start tuning, not count as a passing command. They cannot authorize a command or auto-save a profile. Digital silence and low-SNR room noise remain ineligible. Synthetic PCM tests cover the threshold, but the owner's wall-distance microphone still needs a real trial.
   A greater-than-fourfold swing among quiet-room level reports is classified as an **unstable baseline**. This could be a transient sound or upstream automatic gain settling; Luma does not infer which. It keeps audio untouched, labels the condition distinctly from steady noise/clipping, and asks for a repeat instead of deriving gain from an unreliable baseline.
4. Apply this trial to 16 kHz mono PCM **before** wake recognition and the unrestricted replay decoder. Continue measuring the untouched capture in parallel; decoded phrases and intent results do not determine the signal profile. Any profile change resets partial recognition state. An owner-local button may save clean, measured signal tuning after three acoustic segments even when the recognizer failed every phrase; doing so closes the phrase check, does not declare it passed, and still requires a real-world before/after test. A fully passed check also saves the numeric profile automatically. Provide a local “Use untouched mic audio” escape hatch that leaves saved HAT gain unchanged.
   Start a saved gain profile on the **first captured frame** after a reset. Ramping up from 1× over the first second can leave the short “Hey Luma” wake phrase almost untreated. A sudden loud frame still lowers gain immediately to keep its peak below the processor ceiling; recovery after that frame remains gradual.
   The gain limiter now measures the **post-filter** peak, not just the raw frame. Otherwise a high-pass filter can emit a frame-boundary transient into a newly quiet raw frame and clip it when gain rises. A synthetic crossing test verifies that this transient remains below the processor ceiling before Vosk receives it.
5. If the guided check changes the ReSpeaker's **hardware** capture gain, discard the old room floor and spoken-sample statistics and restart the quiet-room interval. Measurements at different hardware gains cannot support one SNR or digital-gain decision. Keep the bounded three-adjustment session limit.
   A quiet raw level alone is insufficient reason to raise HAT gain: compare it with the measured room floor even when both values are very small. If speech is not at least three times the baseline, amplify neither; explain placement/noise instead. This avoids chasing low-SNR noise with repeated gain resets.
   An automatic HAT **increase** also requires at least three nonzero quiet-room measurements. An absent or all-zero baseline cannot justify raising gain from a faint phrase, because a missing source can resemble a silent room. Use the conservative upper-quartile room level for that comparison; a clipped phrase may still lower gain immediately. Source tests cover zero, insufficient, clean and clipped cases, but the ReSpeaker response still needs on-Pi checking.
   The same reset now applies when the owner moves the manual capture-gain slider during an active check; changing it to its existing value does not waste an adjustment or restart the baseline. Starting a new check uses untouched PCM even if an older saved profile exists. Canceling restores the saved everyday profile **only if the capture path was unchanged**. A successful manual or guided HAT gain change now invalidates the old digital-processing profile, because its clean-room/SNR evidence belonged to a different input level. A route/mute repair at the same gain also invalidates it. If a mixer or gain-file operation fails, Luma rereads gain and capture routing: it preserves the profile only if the entire path is demonstrably unchanged; otherwise it clears the profile, cancels any active check, and asks for a new hardware check. Run the quiet-room check again before applying digital conditioning. Source tests cover these transitions; actual ReSpeaker behavior remains unverified on the Pi.
6. While a tuned profile is being tried, replay the same bounded phrase through both existing constrained and unrestricted decoders on tuned PCM and untouched PCM. Keep only aggregate word/wake-match results and a short-lived, local transcript for troubleshooting. If untouched audio wins on word or positive-wake match at least twice more than tuned audio, turn processing off for the rest of that check and prohibit saving it. A **new false wake on a no-wake control** also vetoes the tuned profile immediately. A learned post-transcription correction must not count as an acoustic win. Reset and reuse the existing two decoders rather than keeping another model in Pi 4 memory; buffer at most seven seconds of microphone frames during this setup-only comparison and discard any gapped utterance. An empty raw transcript is a valid miss, not an API error.
   The bounded setup decode is now one testable step: both recognizers receive **tuned PCM first**, then each is reset and reused for the optional raw comparison, and only then is a candidate intent selected. A synthetic-frame test checks this ordering and confirms an untouched profile does not run a redundant second decode. After the A/B replay, the voice agent discards queued microphone frames recorded during decoding, resets decoder/filter state, and preserves capture failure or EOF markers. Otherwise a slow Pi 4 replay could feed the old phrase tail into the next prompt; on-device dropped-frame and latency checks are still required. This does not replace a real spoken-phrase test on the Pi. The early owner-triggered “Save mic tuning” remains provisional if no negative control has been reached; it explicitly does **not** claim that false wakes are ruled out.

   Once the first three clean acoustic samples establish a candidate, the 0.2.6 phrase check now holds its gain/filter choice fixed. Previously, each new phrase could move the candidate (for example, 2× to 1.75× gain), while the A/B score pooled trials from both profiles and might save a profile that was never consistently tested. The displayed room/SNR measurements continue to update, and later clipping, noise, unstable room sound, or a raw-decoder advantage still disables processing. A hardware capture-gain change discards the frozen candidate and starts a new baseline. Source tests verify these transitions; the actual room and ReSpeaker remain unqualified.

   The transcription A/B score now expands common contractions before counting word edits: “what’s” and “what is” are equivalent acoustic evidence, whether the recognizer includes an apostrophe or not. Previously a correct expansion could score markedly worse than the contracted spelling and contribute to disabling otherwise helpful mic processing. Semantic paraphrases such as “what’s the time” versus “what time is it” remain distinct in this **word** score; intent agreement is still measured separately. This does not retrain the speech model or claim improved recognition on the Pi.

   A complete ten-phrase check no longer **automatically saves** a nontrivial filter solely because the phrases eventually passed. The saved candidate must also have been tested on at least two distinct, successfully recognized command prompts and one successfully recognized no-wake control, with each prompt decoded through both processed and untouched PCM. Otherwise the check can pass, but the persisted audio stays untouched and the UI explains why. This closes a timing hole where a just-measured filter might activate late while the owner rapidly completed the prompts, then be saved despite having little or no A/B evidence. The owner-local early “Save mic tuning” remains an explicit provisional override after clean acoustic samples; its warning about incomplete false-wake qualification remains. Source tests cover both the guarded and successful automatic-save paths; Pi timing and real-room benefit still need testing.

   Paired evidence is **pre-intent**: a post-transcription personal correction cannot count merely because it maps a misheard phrase to the expected command. The unrestricted decoder must independently hear enough of the prompted words (at least a 75% word match), the wake/no-wake behavior must agree with that prompt, and the sample must have a clean level. Only then does a paired raw/tuned observation count toward automatic saving. This does not change learned intent corrections themselves; it prevents them from disguising poor audio recognition during calibration. The threshold is a conservative software guard, not a measured Pi accuracy guarantee.

Software tests use synthetic PCM, corrupt profile inputs, noisy/quiet/clipped examples, a delayed capture handshake, profile persistence and reset, and private-route authorization. They cannot prove recognition gain in the owner's room. On-Pi acceptance must compare the **same phrases** before and after calibration at the normal wall distance, with the microphone meter, dropped-frame count, SNR/quality assessment, and last-heard text visible. If the new profile makes recognition worse, reset it immediately; retain the raw behavior until a measured improvement is confirmed.

The guided check also distinguishes an **all-zero capture stream** from a merely quiet but nonzero microphone after the prompted phrase has waited twelve seconds. It advises checking the selected source or mute state rather than repeatedly increasing hardware gain. This is only a diagnostic conditional on the owner actually speaking; software cannot infer an utterance from digital silence.

The call and owner-voice trials now freeze capture gain and audio-profile resets so one result cannot silently mix processing conditions. Turning the microphone off ends the call trial and discards an active owner-voice trial. Guided calibration may still change gain, but each change restarts its room baseline and discards earlier acoustic measurements. The calibration status now advances a capture revision when gain changes: the live worker sees that as a new audio boundary **even though the session ID is unchanged**, resets both recognizers and its pre-recognizer filter, discards queued speech, and clears per-second room-meter accumulators before measuring the new baseline. Without this, a pre-adjustment meter interval could be submitted as the first post-adjustment quiet-room sample. These are source-tested invariants, not yet Pi-tested audio behavior.

Automatic capture-gain changes have the same partial-failure risk as the manual gain slider: the HAT mixer may change before the gain-file write fails. The calibration endpoint now re-reads the HAT after a failed automatic write. If the path changed or cannot be verified, it clears saved PCM tuning, aborts the check, and leaves a visible instruction to recheck hardware; no old acoustic evidence can pass. If a failed write left the path unchanged, the owner may continue at that level, but auto-adjustment stops retrying for this check and the Voice panel retains a warning. A nominally successful write with unreadable/mismatched capture status also aborts rather than pretending calibration passed. Synthetic failure tests cover these branches; hardware interruption and reboot persistence remain to be tested on the Pi.

The live voice loop now treats the beginning/end of a guided check, call trial, owner-voice trial, and wake-filter mode switch as audio boundaries: both recognizers, the pre-recognizer filter state, acoustic segmenter, wake window, and buffered utterances are reset, and already queued mic frames are discarded while preserving capture-error/EOF markers. Previously the guided-check boundary left the unrestricted recognizer and queued pre-check audio intact, allowing stale words to contaminate the first check or a phrase tail to be interpreted after a check ended. A focused runtime test covers the common reset helper and terminal-marker preservation. The actual timing of these transitions still needs a Pi trial, especially while someone is speaking as a check starts or ends.

### Remaining audio hypotheses to discriminate on hardware

| Stage | Possible cause | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ReSpeaker / PipeWire input | Missing virtual source, wrong capture route, too-low level, HAT gain at limit, clipping, room noise, or dropped frames under CPU load | Mic hardware state, raw meter, room floor, phrase peak, dropped-frame counter |
| Wake / words | Constrained Vosk grammar mishears wake, unrestricted decoder disagrees, echo from the speaker, or PCM conditioning harms words | Display both temporary transcripts during calibration; compare raw vs conditioned utterances before any intent change |
| Piper model | Installed files pass smoke test but worker fails later, second model exhausts Pi RAM, or the model itself has undesirable timbre | Separate sample worker/start/synthesis error and real audible sample through the same warmed service |
| Output | User audio socket unavailable, virtual sink absent, selected physical sink changes, playback command fails, or eSpeak silently chooses another route | Tone and speech route/error plus the actual sound heard by the owner |

None of these are declared physically resolved by desktop tests. The [eSpeak NG manual](https://manpages.debian.org/testing/espeak-ng/espeak-ng.1.en.html) documents its stdin/stdout speech options; its [source](https://github.com/espeak-ng/espeak-ng/blob/master/src/espeak-ng.c) shows that stdout WAV cannot have finalized lengths.

The 0.2.6 capture route now ignores an inherited `PULSE_SOURCE`, always names `luma_mic` unless Luma's own explicit override is set, and connects to the current `luma` user's PipeWire/Pulse socket rather than an unrelated login default. The voice worker checks for a complete PCM frame at least every fifteen seconds. Digital silence **is** a valid frame; a truly stalled stream is reported as `capture_stream_stalled` and the failing voice service is restarted by its existing systemd policy. This is recovery for a dead stream, not automatic proof that the physical mic is audible. The owner must test during normal listening and Sleep/display-off on the Pi; repeated stalls would point back to PipeWire/HAT routing rather than word-calibration settings.

The capture queue now counts a dropped frame when a terminal/error marker evicts buffered audio from a full queue, and also counts an incomplete final PCM frame at EOF. The voice consumer sees the gap, resets its recognizers and refuses to execute from that utterance while preserving the terminal failure marker. It rechecks the drop counter after constrained decoding, after any unrestricted/strict-wake replay, and immediately before posting a command: those stages can take long enough on a busy Pi for the capture thread to overflow **after** the first loop check. A gapped call-test utterance is not counted either. Previously the marker could replace a frame without flagging a gap; a valid-looking prefix might otherwise be interpreted after a broken stream. Focused tests cover queue accounting and reset behavior, but a deliberately interrupted capture and overloaded live-command trial on the Pi are still required to qualify real recovery behavior.

The system image's [PipeWire echo-cancel configuration](../source/system/pipewire/99-luma-echo-cancel.conf) enables WebRTC gain control, noise suppression and voice detection upstream of Luma's own measurements. These may change the level or timing seen by Vosk even when the ReSpeaker mixer remains fixed. [PipeWire's module documentation](https://pipewire.pages.freedesktop.org/pipewire/page_module_echo_cancel.html) also confirms that echo cancellation uses distinct virtual capture/playback nodes and physical streams. This is a **hypothesis**, not a diagnosis of the owner's Pi: compare live raw/processed levels and the actual source/sink links before changing AEC settings. The signed application updater only replaces `/opt/luma` and cannot quietly rewrite the existing user-level PipeWire configuration; any proven image-level fix needs an explicit scoped migration or a later image, with rollback and saved settings preserved.

The source review found a separate, deterministic routing limitation: 0.2.6 replies still prefer the selected physical ALSA sink when available. Such a direct playback does not enter `luma_speaker`, so the echo canceller receives no reference for **Luma's own reply**, even if the reply is audible. Voice settings now reports this route explicitly as `echo_reference_bypassed`, separately from mute/near-zero-volume warnings. This does **not** explain a Zoom call playing on a different device, and does not prove that Luma's own voice actually retriggered the mic. We are not silently moving replies to the virtual sink: PipeWire can accept audio there while its playback stream is mislinked or inaudible. On the Pi, first compare an audible sample on both routes, check the live PipeWire links and post-reply mic/wake behavior, then make a reversible routing choice only if the virtual path works.

If the selected speaker and Piper engine both test successfully yet the voice remains unpleasant, timbre—not a software fallback—is the leading hypothesis. An A/B candidate for the Pi 4's 2 GB memory is the US female [LJ Speech *medium* Piper model](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/ljspeech/medium/MODEL_CARD), whose card lists public-domain training data; the [model file](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/ljspeech/medium/en_US-ljspeech-medium.onnx) is about 63.5 MB (published SHA-256 `6f52a751e2349abe7a76735eb09dc1875298c77ea2342ffd2fef79ff81b87f22`) and its [configuration](https://huggingface.co/rhasspy/piper-voices/blob/main/en/en_US/ljspeech/medium/en_US-ljspeech-medium.onnx.json) specifies 22.05 kHz output. It has not been auditioned or performance-tested on this Pi, so 0.2.6 does not silently replace Kristin or download an unsigned model. If the owner confirms audible Piper but dislikes Kristin, package a separately signed, pinned candidate; load only one model at a time, compare the same sample for voice quality and response latency on the Pi, and ask the owner to choose the default.
