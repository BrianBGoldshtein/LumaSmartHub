# Display design and visual QA

## Current direction

Calendar slide now uses the [complete day timeline](DAY_AGENDA.md), superseding the old three-row agenda slide described below. Four-hour sections, readable overlap columns, separate all-day sections, Google colors, complete-day data and full-detail tap views. Home retains its compact upcoming rows. Packed calendars get more sections, not reduced font sizes or dropped events.

Arcade master grid: the existing responsive `--pixel` is the base spacing unit. `--arcade-cell` is a whole multiple of that unit sized to fit twenty Tetris rows and the preview column. All arcade games use this same full-screen background grid and origin. Tetris uses that background as its only visible grid: its 10×20 board, previews and frame are aligned to whole cells, with no independent overlay grid. Other arcade game frames, score text sizes, control islands and phone-notification spacing use whole base units. Moving sprites retain continuous positions for smooth motion; this is a layout grid, not a forced motion quantizer. Existing dashboard pixel-based spacing/type rules remain in place; this is not a claim that every glyph outline or every legacy component has been snapped to visible cell boundaries.

Game palettes are shared theme tokens (`--game-I/J/L/O/S/T/Z`) used by falling blocks and the other game sprites: crisp pastel arcade colors, muted cool Glass, and warm/desaturated Hearth. Tetromino color roles remain identifiable (cyan, blue, orange, yellow, green, purple, red), but shades match the active theme. Flat tile surfaces replace Tetris-only glossy bevels. Glass/Hearth retain one subtle board grid; the arcade theme extends its exact Tetris cell grid across the background.

One shared layout and spacing system across all three themes. Use the screen for time, weather, and events. Do not add oversized letters, orbital ornaments, decorative date panels, excessive labels, or filler sentences. Labels must not compete with useful content.

Home has a large clock and weather summary above full-width agenda rows. AM/PM is separate from clock digits. Agenda and to-dos use nearly all available space for content, with a restrained heading. Forecast is four columns in landscape and four full-width rows in portrait. Footer controls have reserved space and never cover content.

Use `vmin` sizes to scale consistently with physical display proportions. Main event titles are approximately 37–50 CSS pixels at 1024x768, depending on page/orientation. Agenda titles allow two intentional lines; long content is clamped without shrinking every title. Supporting calendar/location text stays on one line. All time displays use the configured timezone.

Luma Glass: Manrope, charcoal/teal, softly rounded event cards. Hearth: Newsreader for clock and major headings, Manrope for data, warm brown/cream. Neon Grid: Pixelify Sans throughout, square controls, pixel weather glyphs, and a shared integer pixel spacing unit. Google event colors are semantic and persist unchanged across all themes. The glowing filled Luma orb is shared across themes, with no letter or rotating ring. Calendar times and titles use comparable sizes (5.3vmin landscape, 5.8vmin portrait on the agenda).

## Reused assets and data

Smooth themes use Lucide React's consistent SVG icon set. Its ISC/Feather MIT notices ship at `source/frontend/public/licenses/lucide.txt` and in the production build. Fonts are bundled locally through Fontsource; the four font license notices also ship in `public/licenses` and `dist/licenses`. Google Calendar has a five-minute runtime sync worker with cached fallback; OAuth still needs the owner's setup. Open-Meteo polls every fifteen minutes after coordinates are configured, with stale cached forecasts on failure. The preview uses sample data, not the owner's account. DAKboard's agenda provided a reference for aligned rows, prominent times, restrained labels, and calendar-color markers—not copied assets.

## Interludes

Interludes occupy the entire viewport, with no dashboard header, page dots, controls, or enclosing card. Game collision walls have a thin theme-colored boundary: a closed Snake field, top/side walls for brick-breaker, top/bottom rails for Pong, and a side/bottom well for falling blocks. Loss/spawn edges stay open. These mark the playfield, not a decorative outer frame; physics and saved runs are unchanged. Tap anywhere to return home. One scene plays per ambient visit; subsequent visits advance through the theme's sequence. These are generated animations, not video files. Reduced-motion preference freezes motion; timers are cleaned up on exit. Informational pages stay still.

- Neon Grid: randomized falling blocks, paddle rally, Snake, brick-breaker, star flight, pixel bouncing balls.
- Luma Glass: pendulums, luminous colliding balls, brick-breaker, interlocking gears, star flight, paddle rally, breathing glow, falling blocks, Snake.
- Hearth: interlocking gears, pendulums, Snake, warm bouncing balls, sunset/embers, brick-breaker, star flight, falling blocks, paddle rally.

Classic scenes use original geometric artwork and the active theme palette. Snake seeks random apples by shortest-path search, simulates growth to avoid traps, and follows its vacating tail when a route is unsafe; occasional player mistakes and hesitations soften this strategy. Each apple earns ten points. Pong uses ball/paddle collisions and two speed/acceleration-limited predictive bots, with points for missed returns. Brick-breaker uses a smoothly tracking paddle, scores ten per brick, and advances levels after clearing the field. Falling blocks display only their score; line count is internal. All four games preserve state between visits, play only while visible, and respect reduced motion. Neon sprites retain consistent pixel geometry while translating smoothly. Preview individually with `?demo=1&page=ambient&hold=1&scene=snake`, `scene=breaker`, or `scene=rally`.

Brick-breaker has twenty distinct configurations derived from steps, waves, lattices, and ribbons. Each has 32 one-hit bricks (four in each of eight rows), mirrored placement, the same lowest row, and unchanged ball/paddle speeds. This keeps the difficulty baseline consistent rather than escalating with level; geometry can still affect clearance time. A random starting layout is followed by a non-repeating tour of the twenty configurations. Launch positions/directions vary on a fresh run. Clearing levels 1–19 preserves ball position, velocity and score; the next wall enters once the ball is safely below it. Losing the ball OR clearing level 20 ends the run, pauses briefly, then resets score and level to 0 and 1 respectively; best score survives. Existing saves retain their current bricks/score, with legacy levels above 20 capped at 20 until that board ends. Snake resets score after becoming trapped, not after filling the board. Pong resets match scores only after a player loses an eleven-point, win-by-two match; individual missed returns retain match totals. Falling blocks reset only on top-out.

Games pause on scene exit and resume the same state on return. Versioned localStorage checkpoints are written on exit/pagehide and every ten seconds while mounted, not on every frame. Valid saved runs restore after a browser restart; abrupt power loss can lose the most recent ten seconds, and browser storage durability is best-effort. Invalid/incompatible saves are ignored without blocking the dashboard. Game saves contain no calendar or account data. In-memory continuity still works when storage is disabled.

Motion refinement: Snake waits 125–180 ms per cell with occasional extra 60 ms hesitation, interpolating its body between grid positions. After a twelve-move grace period its player occasionally takes a safe detour (3.5% decision probability) or misses a required turn (1.4% of turn decisions), which can cause a real wall/body collision; these are not fixed loss rates. Pong and brick-breaker paddles use speed/acceleration-limited tracking with gradual braking and reversals. Classic scenes render with requestAnimationFrame; sprites retain pixel-shaped geometry without quantized four-unit position jumps. Falling pieces interpolate translations while retaining grid-based collision logic and discrete rotations. New motion fields migrate older saves without resetting runs.

Physics scenes use a shared tested numerical engine. Free disks have mass proportional to area, frictionless elastic impulses, and reflective walls; pairwise impacts conserve linear momentum and kinetic energy. Hanging balls integrate gravity with velocity Verlet and exchange elastic impulses along their permitted tangents. Their fixed supports supply external forces, so total bob-only linear momentum is not conserved. Small substeps and overlap correction control numerical error; this is a visual approximation, not a precision multi-contact physics solver. Reduced-motion settings freeze both scenes. Five physics tests cover unequal-mass oblique impacts, separating contacts, sustained box energy, pendulum energy, and hanging-ball velocity transfer.

Hanging balls begin exactly vertical on every scene entry. Only their initial lengths (215–265 scene units) and angular velocities (random direction, 0.14–0.38 rad/s) are randomized. No random forces or scripted positions are injected after initialization; gravity and contact impulses determine subsequent motion. A regression test checks initial alignment, variation bounds, and evolution without changing string lengths.

The maze is removed. Falling blocks use a ten-by-twenty board, randomized seven-bags, standard piece colors, collision/rotation kicks, locking, and automatic restart. The owner's latest instruction restores **classic line clearing**: every fully occupied row flashes and clears, regardless of color. Autoplay rewards clears and avoids holes/uneven stacks; it no longer rewards matching colors. Normal single/double/triple/four-line clears award 100/300/500/800 points. Legacy color-match saves retain board/score; if more than four accumulated full rows clear at the next lock, that one-time clear awards at most 800 points while counting all lines, with safe checkpoint recovery. The board persists between visits. It is not an interactive or guideline-certified game.

All four games share `GameFrame`: the same external score rail, physical font size, bold theme typeface, spacing, and two-pixel boundary stroke. Snake adds best score and wallbreaker adds level in the muted secondary position; falling blocks show score only, and Pong shows its two-player score. SVG scaling no longer changes score text size between games. Falling blocks uses a compact five-piece next queue beside the well; no duplicate in-field score labels. At 1024x768, all twelve theme/game combinations were checked for matching header typography, scores above the field, and viewport fit.

## Review URLs

Latest pacing: falling-block autoplay waits 2–4 simulation ticks (190–380 ms at the 95 ms cadence) after each spawn, plus a 35% chance of one extra tick between rotation/lateral inputs. Thinking/steering gravity is `.09 × multiplier` cells/tick; committed fall is `.105 × multiplier`. The shared multiplier is `min(4, 2.5 + score/12000)`, so both speeds increase gradually while thinking time stays fixed. At score zero, drift is approximately 2.37 cells/s and committed fall 2.76 cells/s; drop remains only one-sixth faster than drift. The handoff never applies both gravity rates in one tick. Fractional gravity/countdowns persist in checkpoints and collisions still check every crossed row.

Owner speed freeze: the subsequent `.095` experiment was explicitly rejected and reverted. Do not retune Tetris drift, committed fall, thinking cadence or score ramp unless the owner explicitly requests it.

Pong uses a shared0.78 tempo factor for ball and paddle speeds; acceleration scales by its square. Serves travel at171.6–187.2 scene units/s, grow2.5% per return, and cap at296.4. Reaction/tracking/spin are adjusted with the same tempo to preserve coherent motion. Version-three checkpoints migrate older ball/paddle tempos once without changing scores, positions or flight direction; repeated reloads do not repeatedly slow play.

Pong player behavior: only the player receiving the ball tracks its landing point. After a return, the outgoing player's short follow-through brakes at the existing acceleration limit before the receiver starts; the inactive paddle stays where it finished, with no automatic recentering. Movement remains smooth and mutually exclusive per simulation substep. Each incoming shot gets a fresh placement offset, approximately71–186 ms reaction delay at the current tempo, and bounded tracking speed; a6% chance of an overambitious edge placement can cause a real miss (not a fixed loss probability). Serves vary in height, angle and speed. Paddle contact position plus paddle-motion spin determine outgoing angles, bounded away from horizontal/vertical stagnation. Random choices happen only on serves/returns, never as frame-by-frame jitter or mid-flight trajectory changes. Shot intent/timing is checkpointed and older saves preserve match progress. Seeded simulations verify varied angles, legal motion and multiple genuinely scored points; this is sampled coverage, not a mathematical guarantee against every possible long rally.

Wallbreaker paddle attention: continuously follow the ball's horizontal position with a short 120 ms look-ahead, through both rising and falling motion. Latest tuning lowers maximum paddle speed from 340 to 280 scene units/s and raises available acceleration from 1200 to 1800 units/s². A 65 ms aim filter softens target jumps after ricochets, with matched position/velocity gains (6/24) for a damped response. The paddle also eases to rest during a lost-ball pause. No height-gated stops, idle recentering, or exact future-intercept targeting. It may naturally pause near an edge or when aligned; no artificial oscillation is added just to force movement. Filtered aim is saved; older checkpoints initialize it from their paddle position. Ball speed, scoring, and level rules remain unchanged, and Pong retains its own tuning. This supersedes the earlier wait-until-descending behavior and original 340/1200 controller.
